Advanced Dental Implants in Kochi: Zygomatic & Maxillofacial Implants

Advanced implant treatment extends beyond routine tooth replacement. This page covers dental implants for complex cases, including digital planning, bone augmentation, zygomatic implants and craniomaxillofacial implant solutions for carefully selected patients.

When Implants Are Recommended

  • Single missing tooth, without needing to cut down neighbouring teeth for a bridge
  • Multiple missing teeth, as a fixed alternative to a removable partial denture
  • Full arch replacement, anchoring a complete set of teeth securely
  • Failing or unstable existing dentures

Advanced Dental Implant Planning

Assessment starts with a clinical examination and three dimensional imaging such as CBCT to evaluate bone volume, implant position and the anatomy around the planned site. Digital planning can be used to assess the ideal position before surgery. Standard implants, grafting or advanced implant solutions are selected according to the anatomy and prosthetic requirements of the case.

Zygomatic and Craniomaxillofacial Implants

Patients with severe loss of upper jaw bone may be considered for zygomatic implants, which use the zygomatic bone as an anchorage site when conventional implant support is limited. For complex craniofacial and maxillofacial defects, patient-specific implants can be designed from CT data and produced to match the individual anatomy.

Patient Specific Maxillofacial Implant Design

For selected reconstructive cases with significant bone loss after trauma, tumour surgery or other structural defects, patient-specific implants can be digitally planned from CT data and designed to follow the exact contour of the defect.

Frequently Asked Questions

Am I a candidate for dental implants?

Most adults with adequate jawbone and healthy gums are candidates. Where bone volume is limited, bone grafting can often create a suitable foundation. A clinical exam and imaging confirm suitability in your specific case.

How long does the implant process take?

A single implant typically takes three to six months from placement to final crown, allowing time for the implant to integrate with bone (osseointegration). Timelines vary with bone quality, the number of implants, and whether grafting is needed.

Are implants painful?

Placement is done under local anaesthesia and is generally well tolerated, with manageable post-operative discomfort similar to a tooth extraction. Most people return to normal activity within a day or two.

What is a patient-specific implant?

For complex jaw defects, after trauma or tumour removal for example, implants can be custom-designed from a CT scan and 3D-printed to match a patient's exact anatomy, rather than using a generic off-the-shelf shape.

How long do dental implants last?

With good oral hygiene and regular review, implants commonly last 15–20+ years, and often for life. Longevity depends more on maintenance and bite forces than on the implant material itself.
