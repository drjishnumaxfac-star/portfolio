Wisdom Tooth Removal: Simple and Impacted Extractions

Wisdom teeth, or third molars, often need to be removed when there isn't enough room for them to come through properly, or when they cause recurrent pain, infection, or damage to the tooth next to them.

When Removal Is Needed

Common reasons for removing a wisdom tooth include recurrent gum infection around a partially erupted tooth, impaction against the neighbouring tooth, cyst formation around an unerupted tooth, and orthodontic reasons such as crowding. Not every wisdom tooth needs to come out. A fully erupted, well positioned, cleanable tooth can often be left in place and simply monitored.

The Needle-Free Anesthesia Option

For suitable cases, local anesthesia can be delivered using a needle free jet injector, which uses a narrow, high pressure stream to deliver the anesthetic through the skin surface rather than a needle prick. This is a genuine option for patients with needle anxiety and for simpler extractions. More complex impacted extractions often still need conventional block anesthesia to achieve adequate depth and duration of numbness, and which approach is appropriate is decided case by case at the consultation.

Recovery

Some swelling and discomfort in the first few days after extraction is normal and manageable with the advice and medication given after the procedure. Ice packs, a soft diet for the first day or two, and avoiding vigorous rinsing or straws in the initial period support normal healing. A follow up review is scheduled to check the extraction site.

If a wisdom tooth is causing pain or has been flagged on an X-ray, a focused assessment will clarify whether removal is needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is needle-free anesthesia and is it as effective as a needle?

It delivers local anesthetic through a narrow, high pressure jet instead of a needle, which suits many simpler extractions and patients with needle anxiety well. More complex impacted extractions may still need a conventional needle based block for adequate depth of numbness, and this is decided individually at consultation.

Is every wisdom tooth removal painful?

Discomfort varies with how simple or complex the extraction is. The procedure itself is done under adequate anesthesia so it should not be painful during the extraction, and some soreness afterward, managed with the advice and medication given, is normal.

How long is recovery after wisdom tooth removal?

Simple extractions often settle within a few days. More involved impacted extractions can take a week or a little longer for swelling and discomfort to fully resolve, with the extraction site itself continuing to heal over several weeks.

Can all four wisdom teeth be removed at the same time?

In many cases yes, particularly when several teeth need removal for similar reasons, but this depends on an individual assessment of each tooth's position and complexity, and is discussed at consultation.

How do I know if my wisdom tooth is impacted?

An impacted wisdom tooth is one that hasn't come through properly, often because there isn't enough space or it is angled against the neighbouring tooth. This is usually identified through clinical examination and an X-ray, which also shows its position relative to nearby teeth and nerves.
