Clear Aligners: Straighten Teeth Without Metal Braces

Clear aligners are a series of removable, nearly invisible trays that gradually move teeth into a planned final position. For patients who want straighter teeth without visible metal brackets, they are one of the most requested options in the clinic today.

Who Clear Aligners Suit

Aligners work well for mild to moderate crowding, spacing between teeth, and several types of bite misalignment. They are commonly used in adults and older teenagers who want a treatment option that is easy to remove for eating and cleaning. Not every case is suitable. Severe skeletal discrepancies, some complex rotations, and certain bite problems still respond better to fixed braces or, in more significant skeletal cases, to orthognathic surgery. A clinical and radiographic assessment before starting treatment is what decides suitability, not preference alone.

How Treatment Works

Treatment begins with a digital scan or impression of the teeth, used to plan the full sequence of tooth movement from the current position to the target result. A series of clear trays is then produced, each worn for roughly one to two weeks before moving to the next in the sequence. Trays are meant to be worn close to full time, generally twenty to twenty two hours a day, removed only for eating, drinking anything other than water, and brushing.

What To Expect During Treatment

Mild pressure or soreness for a day or two after starting a new tray is normal and expected. Review appointments are scheduled periodically to check that tooth movement is tracking the plan and to issue the next set of trays. Total treatment time depends on how much correction is needed, ranging from a few months for minor spacing to well over a year for more involved cases. A retainer is required after active treatment to hold the result, since teeth can drift back without one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do clear aligners hurt?

Most patients feel mild pressure or tenderness for the first day or two after switching to a new tray in the series. This is expected as the tooth begins to move and typically settles quickly, unlike the more persistent discomfort sometimes associated with tightening fixed braces.

How long does clear aligner treatment take?

Treatment length depends on the complexity of the case. Minor spacing or crowding can be corrected in a few months, while more involved bite corrections can take a year or longer. A personalised timeline is given after the initial scan and treatment plan.

Can everyone use clear aligners instead of braces?

No. Aligners suit mild to moderate cases well, but some rotations, significant crowding, and skeletal bite discrepancies still need fixed braces or, in more severe cases, orthognathic surgery. An assessment determines which approach fits your bite.

How many hours a day do I need to wear the trays?

Aligners need to be worn close to full time, generally twenty to twenty two hours a day, to keep treatment on schedule. They come out for eating, for drinking anything other than water, and for brushing and flossing.

What happens after treatment finishes?

A retainer is required once active tooth movement is complete. Teeth continue to have some tendency to shift over time, and a retention plan, worn as advised, is what keeps the corrected position stable.
