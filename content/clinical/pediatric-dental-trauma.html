Pediatric Dental Trauma: Emergency Care for Children's Tooth Injuries

Falls, sports, and everyday play are common causes of injury to a child's teeth and mouth. Some of these injuries are genuinely time sensitive, and what happens in the first hour can affect the long term outcome for the tooth.

Common Injuries in Children

Dental trauma in children ranges from a chipped or fractured tooth, to a tooth pushed out of position (luxation), to a tooth knocked out completely (avulsion), along with injuries to the lips, gums, and soft tissue of the mouth. The approach differs depending on whether a baby tooth or a permanent tooth is involved, and on how much time has passed since the injury.

What To Do Immediately

For a knocked out permanent tooth, handle it by the crown and not the root, rinse it gently with water or milk if visibly dirty without scrubbing the root surface, and try to reinsert it into the socket if possible, or store it in milk or saline while getting to a clinic. Time matters here, and care within thirty to sixty minutes gives the best chance of the tooth surviving. A knocked out baby tooth is generally not reimplanted, since doing so can risk the developing permanent tooth underneath, but it should still be assessed.

Treatment Approach

Assessment includes a clinical examination and, where needed, an X-ray to check the tooth, the surrounding bone, and the developing permanent tooth if a baby tooth is involved. Depending on the injury, treatment may include repositioning and splinting a displaced tooth, restoring a chipped tooth, or monitoring a tooth over time for changes in colour or sensitivity that can indicate the nerve has been affected. Follow up review is part of the plan for most trauma cases, sometimes over several months.

If your child has had a dental injury, contact the clinic promptly rather than waiting to see if symptoms settle.

Frequently Asked Questions

My child's permanent tooth got knocked out, what do I do?

Handle the tooth by the crown, avoid touching the root, and try to gently reinsert it into the socket if your child will tolerate it. If that is not possible, keep it in milk or saline and get to a clinic as quickly as possible, ideally within thirty to sixty minutes, since prompt care meaningfully improves the outcome.

Should a knocked out baby tooth be put back in?

Generally no. Reimplanting a baby tooth can risk damage to the permanent tooth developing beneath it. The area should still be examined to check for any injury to the surrounding gum, bone, or the developing tooth.

How urgent is treatment after a dental injury?

It depends on the injury. A knocked out permanent tooth is a genuine emergency where minutes matter. A chipped tooth or minor soft tissue injury is less time critical but should still be assessed within a day or two to rule out deeper damage.

Will a chipped tooth need a root canal?

Not always. It depends on how much of the tooth structure was lost and whether the injury reached the nerve. Many chipped teeth are managed with a simple restoration, while deeper fractures that expose or affect the nerve may need further treatment.

Can trauma to a baby tooth affect the adult tooth growing underneath?

It can, depending on the type and severity of the injury and the child's age at the time. This is one reason even apparently minor trauma to a baby tooth is worth having assessed rather than left unchecked.
